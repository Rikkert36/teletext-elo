Icoon cards — dropping the tiers, and finding the colourway

Legend cards currently come in three colourways, one per tier. This document asks two questions: which of those three gets closest to the FIFA 19 Icon look we are after, and what else is still on the table once we commit to a single uniform card.

Live against the five cards asked for — Luuk Reinders (64, bronze), Charlie Cottrell (68, silver), Daryll Sardjoe (70, silver), Rakesh Badal (84, gold) and Bart Rutten (85, gold rare). Real photos, real overalls, pulled from the pool endpoint. The card CSS on this page is a faithful copy of src/styles/card.css; the proposals sit on top of it as switchable classes, so every plate below is the actual rendering and not a mockup. The shape is fixed — nothing here proposes a shield or a notch.

Proposals

The target

What the reference actually is

Six things characterise the reference card, and it is worth separating them because only some are ours to take.

  • A near-white card. Nothing on the surface is darker than about 75% lightness. It is pale in a way none of our three currently are.
  • Two zones, warm and cool. The card is not uniformly gold and not uniformly grey — there is a warm ivory light zone (upper right) and a cool grey-taupe shadow zone (left flank and lower edge). This is the single most misread thing about these cards.
  • The portrait is a cut-out. No rectangle, no background, no edge. The player is a shape on the card, rendered as a warm-grey lithograph with visible print grain.
  • Broad soft grey streaks across the surface — the same idea as our --facets, but grey rather than white and far softer.
  • Gold is an accent, not a ground. The only saturated gold is the piping, the crest and the type. The card body is never gold.
  • The card is quiet and the player is loud. No rays, no glow, no radiating anything. Everything on the surface either is the player or is information about them.

Question one

Which tier's colourway gets us closest?

Four things differ between the bronze, silver and gold legend card, and nothing else does. Taken one at a time against the reference:

1

The ground — the card's own surface

silver's restraint, gold's warmth

The four-stop gradient that shows in the bottom band and the top-left corner. Shown here at the gradient's darkest stop, which is what carries the character.

brons #c19b76
s 39% · L 61% — too saturated and too dark by a distance
zilver #b3bcc5
s 12% · L 73% — nearest in value and saturation, but cool with no warm zone at all
goud #c3b18e
s 25% · L 66% — right hue direction, but reads as tan card stock rather than shadow
reference ≈ #d3d5d2
s ~3% · L 83% — paler and flatter than any of the three

This is the one place silver genuinely wins, and it wins on the numbers rather than on the hue: the reference's shading is a desaturated grey at very high lightness, and silver is the only one of the three in that neighbourhood. Bronze and gold both read as coloured card stock, which the reference never does.

But silver alone is wrong too, because the reference is not a cool card — it has a warm ivory zone that silver has no equivalent of, and our ink, wash and accents are all warm. A cool-only ground fights every one of them. The answer is a two-zone ground: silver's lightness and saturation, gold's warm light zone. That is proposal 9 below.

2

The coloured ray — --shard-lo

blue-grey — the reference has no cool accent anywhere
goud #c6a04a
this is almost exactly the reference's piping gold
reference ≈ #c9a54e
the only saturated colour on the whole card

Gold's ray colour is the right colour — it is within a couple of percent of the reference's piping. The problem is what we are doing with it. The reference puts that gold on a line, as an edge and a crest; we have it fanning out of the bottom of the card as rays of light. Radiating light is the loudest thing on our icoon and the reference has none of it.

So: keep gold's hue, move it from the rays to an edge. Proposals 1 and 8.

3

The white ray — --shard-hi

none of them — this was never a choice

White at .66 / .72 / .78 across bronze / gold / silver. It is a compensation value, set so the white still reads against each ground, not a design decision — which is why the three are indistinguishable. The reference's surface texture is soft broad grey, never white slivers. If the fan goes, this goes with it; if the fan stays, pick any of the three, it will not matter.

4

The wash over the photo — --tier-a / --tier-b

goud, clearly

The colour multiplied over the desaturated photo. Shown top stop → bottom stop.

brons
swings to orange at the bottom — a hue shift down the frame
zilver
warm to cool in one photo, which is the worst of the three
goud
warm at both ends — no hue shift, only a value change
reference
flatter still, and closer to neutral than gold is

The reference portraits are one continuous warm grey from hairline to shoulder. Gold is the only one of ours that does not shift hue partway down the face, because silver and bronze are forced to start warm (to keep the sepia grade alive) and then end somewhere else. Gold wins outright, and should be flattened further still.

Verdict: gold, with the ground borrowed from silver. Gold takes the accent colour and the photo wash on merit. Silver takes nothing on hue — it wins the ground purely for being the palest and flattest of the three, which is the property the reference has and none of ours do. Bronze wins nothing at all: it is the most saturated and the darkest, and the reference is neither.

Question two

What else is on the table

Nine changes, none of which touch the outline. Every one is a switch in the bar at the top of this page — turn them on one at a time against the plate below, which is the gold colourway as it stands today.

live

Gold base + whatever is switched on

#ChangeWhy, and what it costs
1Drop the shard fan The largest single move, and the one that gets the most reference in return. The reference surface has no rays. The fan exists only because the portrait mask clears the bottom band and the corner and something had to fill them — but the reference fills that same area with plain card and information, which is quieter and more expensive-looking. Cost: the bottom third goes bare, which is why #9 and the open question at the bottom of this page matter.
2Grey the facets The two diagonal wedges are white at .16 and .11. On a saturated gold card they read as metal; on a near-white card they read as a smudge on the print. The reference does have broad diagonal streaks, but they are soft grey. Same idea, wrong colour. Cost: none — the same values apply to the ordinary tiers unchanged, since this only moves on the icoon.
3Cut the sheen to .05 The 115deg white band at .17 is calibrated for foil. Across a pale card it is a visible diagonal stripe with nothing to justify it. Cost: the icoon stops looking foiled, which is arguably correct — the reference reads as printed board, not foil.
4Cut the portrait out The biggest gap between us and the reference after the rays. Their photos have no background and no rectangle — the player is a shape lying on the card. We already ship a per-player outline mask for the empty slot, rendered at the card's exact 5:7 crop, so this is a mask swap and nothing more. Cost: mask quality is now load-bearing on cards you own, not just on ones you don't. Look hard at Charlie and Daryll below — the tool's output around hair and shoulders is where this will or won't hold up.
5Lithograph grade Lighter, flatter, grainier — contrast .92 / brightness 1.14 with print grain over the top. The reference is a halftone illustration, not a photograph: paper-white highlights, warm-grey midtones, no gloss. Cost: darker photos in the pool lose detail. Worth checking against the whole legend list, not just these five.
6Warm and lift the ink #594d2c → #7c6a3c. Ours is a dark olive-brown; the reference's is a lighter, more golden bronze. On a paler ground the darker ink starts to read as black type rather than as metal. Cost: contrast drops — check it at card--sm in the album grid before committing.
7A 1px gold rule inside the edge Not a shape change — a border on the rounded rectangle we already have. This is where the gold from #2 should go, and after the pale ground it is the strongest single icon cue available to us. Cost: it is the one proposal that risks looking like piping drawn around the wrong silhouette. Judge it on the light plate below, not on black.
8Lighten the drop shadow 0 3px 12px rgba(0,0,0,.6) is tuned for a dark card. Under a near-white one it reads as grime. Cost: none.
9The two-zone ground The conclusion of question one: silver's lightness and saturation, gold's warm zone, as a warm radial in the upper right over a near-neutral body that shades to grey-taupe at the lower left. Cost: a radial plus a linear where there was one linear. Nothing structural.
Ruled out: setting the name in caps. The reference is unambiguous about it — RIVALDO, not Rivaldo — and it would have been a second free thing separating an icoon from an ordinary card. It is off the table anyway: the casing on the card does not change. Mixed case, as it is now, on every card in the set.

Still open

Three things this document does not decide

  • What fills the bottom third. Once the rays go, the band under the portrait is bare card with a name on it. The reference fills the same space with six stats and two rules, and we have deliberately never printed stats. But a legend is an inactive player, and there is one fact about them that an active card could never carry and that is genuinely the point of the tier — how many games they played, or the years they were around. One line, in the ink, under the name. Worth trying before we conclude the space should stay empty.
  • Whether anything should be saturated. The reference's only real colour is the flag and the crest, and it is what stops the card being monotonous. We have no nations and no clubs. Either we accept a card with no colour on it at all, or we find our own equivalent — and I do not think a made-up crest is it.
  • Whether the cut-out survives the whole pool. Five cards is enough to judge the colourway and not enough to judge the masks. That needs the full legend list rendered at album size in one go.
