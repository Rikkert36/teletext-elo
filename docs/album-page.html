Why the fold is the halfway line

A foosball table is portrait and a spread is landscape, so the drawing has to be turned one way or the other, and the choice is not free. Turning the table — rods running down the page, goals at the two fore-edges — puts eight vertical lines behind six cards and reads as a pitch again. Keeping the table upright and turning the crop gives the arrangement above: goal end at the head, rods running across, halfway line at the foot.

That makes the gutter the long axis of the field. The centre of the goal, the penalty spot, the centre spot and the middle man on every odd rod all fall on x = 34, which is the fold — so the fold is doing the one thing a fold can do for a drawing, which is be the axis the markings are set out from. The two halves are not mirrors: the handles alternate, so each leaf carries a genuinely different half of one table. And it has to work that way, because leaves turn independently and nothing may span the gutter as one element. Both pages are handed the same marks and their viewBoxes do the cropping, split at 34.

Half a table, four rods

Half, because a spread is half. The four rods are the first half of the real 1-2-3-5-5-3-2-1 order, at the real spacing — the surface is 120 × 68, so this is 68 across by 60 down and every figure below is in those units rather than in pixels. It can be checked against a tape measure instead of against taste.

The left/right alternation is the real one: a rod belongs to whoever's side its handle comes out on, which is why the 3-bar in the middle is the opponent's and reaches in from the other margin. The handles are what stop this reading as a football pitch — they run out past the trim on both fore-edges, and they are the only marks in the drawing that no pitch has. The men are bars across the rod rather than dots, because that is a foosball figure seen from above; the near team's are filled and the far team's outlined, since one ink cannot do two strips.

Drawn, not plotted

The first version of this was exact SVG geometry, and exact was wrong. A perfect vector diagram on aged stock reads as a screenshot of something else pasted under the page; what the album wants is the drawing a printer would have made for it. So every line here is stroked by hand: each primitive is a parametric curve sampled with a smooth two-frequency wobble along its normal, laid down twice with different phase, and corners overshoot the way a hand's do.

It is the same argument the checklist ticks already won — three drawn paths rather than a glyph, hashed from the player id so the mark is stable. The wobble here is seeded per element from a fixed table, so the drawing is identical on every render and on every page. A field that re-sketched itself when the leaf turned would be an animation, not a watermark. The Hand slider above is a tuning control only; it is a constant in the real thing.

The end wall is hatched rather than filled, which is what a section is in a technical drawing, and it is also the cheapest way to keep a solid black band from landing directly under the running head. Everything is the press's brown #6b5325 and not --graphite: the two-hands rule on the checklist page says graphite is the reader's, and the reader did not draw this. It came off the press with the paper.

What is still open

The ink. The slider starts at .20. Below about .12 the rods stop being legible under a filled row of cards and the drawing reads as a stain; above about .30 the hatched wall starts competing with the head rule. The band worth arguing over is narrow.

The tab. Off by default, because it has not been earned yet. The selector runs the three candidates: a spread number, which is the one figure nothing else on the page states and is honestly true of both halves; the rating range, which the head already says four centimetres away; and the folio, which the foot already says. Only the first says something new, and it is still a second piece of furniture on a page that was deliberately emptied.

What it costs. Two <svg> per spread, built once from a table of four rods and about twenty marks, at z-index: 0 under .album__slots. No library — the wobble is thirty lines of parametric sampling, which is well inside the rule that keeps framer-motion and friends out of this project.
