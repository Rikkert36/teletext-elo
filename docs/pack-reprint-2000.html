On the shelf

The real test. At --album-card-w the packet is about 118px across and every one of these is read at a glance from four feet away — which is the whole argument for the numeral being this big. The set-completion packet is on the right: same composition, no dominant hue, white board and a gold rule, the hexagon where the digit goes.

Anatomy

  • Crimp, 0–15%. Ribbed silver, both ends. Nothing is printed inside it — ink does not survive a fold, and keeping the panel clear of it is what makes the ends read as sealed rather than as a border.
  • Headband, top 14%. Cream board, gold rule, black rule, pixel rail. The wordmark over it at 94% wide, overlapping the rules by about a third of its height. Nothing else up here.
  • Rod, 25–56%. One rod, one red man, one blue man, the ball off the blue man's foot with speed lines behind it and two sparks. It crosses the full width and runs off both edges, so the packet is a crop out of a longer table.
  • Numeral, 44–88%. Overlaps the rod band deliberately: the digit is in front of the artwork, not below it. Cream keyline, black outline, gold halftone face, hard offset shadow.
  • Score rail, bottom 11%. Bead counter on a chrome wire, three gold beads slid across. It closes the composition where a barcode block would have.
  • Over everything: grain, the inset cream/black keyline frame, the foil bulge, and a half-strength sheen.

What was decided, and why

A — the badge comes off the front

Wordmark only

The full emblem carries players, ball, scoreboard and pitch. On a wrapper that already has players, a ball and a score rail printed on it, the badge is the same sentence said twice — and it was eating the top 45% of the packet to say it. In its place is the new rik-dev-wordmark.png: raked italic, gold face with a black keyline and a green halo, and the ball already trailing off the v. That last detail is why the wrapper's own ball could be handed to the blue man's foot instead — the mark supplies one, so the artwork does not have to repeat it centred under the logo.

It is printed over the cream band rather than inside it: 94% of the packet wide against a band 14% tall, so it laps the gold and black rules and touches the field. That overlap is the single most period-correct thing on the wrapper, and the mark's own black keyline is what lets it happen without a plate underneath. The emblem is not retired — it stays on the card back and in the album, where there is room for it.

B — the count

The number is the artwork

It runs from the rod to the score rail and is roughly three times the height it was. Four passes, in order: a hard navy shadow offset down-right, a cream keyline, a black outline, and a gold face with a dot screen in it. No caption anywhere — the digit is the whole statement, exactly as PackFace already has it. The reason it can be this big is docket A: the badge was occupying the space.

C — table soccer, printed

One rod, two men, one ball

Not a second logo. A single rod crossing the whole panel and running off both edges, so the packet reads as a crop out of a longer table; a red man and a blue man on it; the ball leaving the blue man's foot with speed lines behind it. Under all of it, the pitch markings at 17% — halfway line, centre circle, two goal boxes — which is what keeps the field from being a plain colour. Flat fills and heavy black keylines throughout, one highlight and one shade per figure. It is drawn like a screen print, and it should be recognisable as foosball with the type covered up.

D — the printing

Six imperfections, on purpose

Halftone dots on a 5px grid in the shadows and a coarser 7px screen bleeding from the top; hard-edged burst rays rather than a soft glow; diagonal cream-and-gold stripes at uneven spacing across the shoulder; feTurbulence displacement on the wordmark and the numeral so their edges wobble the way ink on foil does; fractal grain over the whole panel at 17% overlay; and the crimps ribbed rather than smooth. Nothing here is smooth by accident — the previous wrapper's problem was that a clean radial gradient reads as a screen, and no amount of colour fixes that.

E — what survives from the current wrapper

The object, not the print

The tooth mask, --crimp, --pack-w/ --pack-h, the sheen sweep, the side bulge and the three-pass drop-shadow are packopen.css untouched — the tear animation and the torn halves therefore need no change at all, since they clip whatever is painted inside them. The sheen is at about half its old strength, because a hard white sweep sells foil over near-black and looks like a filter over cream.

F — the two packets that are not 1/3/5

Icoon and gift

The set-completion packet keeps its rule: white board, gold rule, gold ends, and the hexagon in the numeral's slot at the numeral's size. It takes the same composition — burst, rod, rail, keyline — with no dominant hue and cream where the colour would be, so it is visibly the rarest thing on the shelf without being a fourth size colour. A forced packet keeps the existing flame hue as its dominant colour and needs nothing else; an odd-sized gift keeps fallbackHue, which drives --dom and the two shades derived from it.

Porting it

packFoil.ts shrinks: the four foil variables become --dom, --dom-lt, --dom-dk off one hue, and --gold/--cream/--navy are constants on .pack. --pack-mark stays as the mechanism but points at rik-dev-wordmark.png instead of the emblem, so utils/brand.ts gains a second export beside rikDevMark — and a second ratio constant, because this asset is trimmed to 2172×724 and wants aspect-ratio: 3 / 1 rather than the emblem's 320 / 256. At 94% of a 250px packet it draws at 235 CSS px wide against 2172 of source, so it has room to spare on a HiDPI screen and could be re-cut smaller if the bundle cares.

PackFace.tsx grows from two spans to the panel's layer stack plus the two symbols. Both symbols want to live once in the app shell rather than per packet, since the opener can have five packets on screen at a time and the torn halves duplicate the whole face. IcoonMark stays as it is, moved into the numeral's slot with an added black keyline so it matches the digits' build.

One caveat worth writing down: filter resolves before mask on the same element, which is why every filter here is on a child (.art svg, the numeral's <g>) and never on .pack itself.
