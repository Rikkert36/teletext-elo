rik-dev — wrapper, second pass

The packet has no composition

Not a list of effects this time. The problem with the wrapper is structural: it is one centred logo with a number under it, floating on a gradient. Retro packs are not decorated — they are zoned. A header, a field, a footer; each zone owns one job, and the decoration lives inside the zones rather than on top of a blank front. Five complete fronts below, same geometry, same hues, same badge asset.

What is actually wrong

The badge is 84% wide

It fills the front edge to edge, so there is no margin, no zone and nothing for it to sit on. Every pack in the reference set runs its logo at 50–65% with air around it.

Everything is centred

Badge centred, number centred, field centred. One axis, no tension. A composition needs at least one thing that is not on the middle line.

The count is a caption

A digit floating on foil reads as a UI label. In print it always sits in something — a bar, a burst, a banner — which is what turns it into packaging.

Nothing says what is inside

The wrapper is entirely brand. A card pack shows its subject: a player, a rod, a ball. The album is full of silhouettes already.

A — kopband

Header band and field

The colour stops being a pool and becomes a zone: a solid band across the top third holding the badge, a chrome rule under it, then near-black film with the count set large. Two zones, one rule, done. This is the layout almost every real pack uses, and it is the only one here that would survive being printed on actual film.

It buys the badge a margin for the first time — 62% instead of 84% — and the count grows to 40% of the pack width because it now has a field to itself instead of sharing one. The word under it is the only text on the front and it is doing the job the colour used to do badly.

B — speler

The rod man is the artwork

A pack of cards shows its subject. Ours never has. The figure is the badge's own rod man, blown up past the edges of the film, ghosted into the foil at 30% and lit by the packet's own glow — so it is not an illustration pasted on, it is the foil embossed. A chrome rod runs edge to edge behind him, which is the same steel as the seals.

The count moves off the centre line into the bottom-right corner, which is the only thing on any of these fronts that breaks the symmetry, and the front is immediately better for it. The badge drops to 58% and finally has somewhere to sit.

Reads at 116px
Yes — the silhouette is one shape, which is what small sizes want.

C — diagonaal

One chevron across the middle

A hard band at −13° with the count inside it and the badge sitting above. This is the most literally period of the five — it is what every sports card brand did between about 1991 and 1996 — and the diagonal does the job the centred layout cannot: it splits the front into two unequal shapes, so the eye has somewhere to go.

The fine vertical ribbing in the band is deliberate and is the one place stripes are defensible: they are inside a shape, at a size you read as a printed surface rather than as a pattern on the film. Outside the band the foil stays plain.

D — blindfoelie

Struck, not printed

The opposite bet. The colour pool goes entirely; the film is near-black with only a breath of hue in it, the badge is blind-stamped — desaturated, half opacity, with a hard shadow underneath so it reads as pressed into the foil rather than inked onto it — and the only colour on the front is one bar at the foot with the count knocked out of it in dark.

This is the direction that ages best and the one that fights the badge least. It is also the least retro of the five, which is worth saying plainly: it is 2010s premium, not 1994. It is here because the icoon packet already works this way and proves the language.

Reads at 116px
The bar does; the blind badge nearly vanishes. Needs a stronger stamp at shelf size.

E — explosie

All of 1994 at once

Full-bleed ray burst behind an oversized badge, an arched chrome banner cutting the front in half, and the count in a twelve-point star below it. Nothing is held back. It is included because a set of directions without a maximum is not a set — and because it is the only one that makes the other four look measured rather than timid.

It is also the one that eats the badge. The burst and the banner both compete with it, and at 116px the arch and the star collide. If this is the direction, the badge has to be redrawn for the wrapper rather than reused from the card back.

If it were mine

B, with A's discipline. The rod man is the only one of the five that adds a reason for the wrapper to exist rather than a style for it to be in, and it is the only one that survives being shrunk to a card's width without losing what it is. A's two-zone split is the right skeleton to hang it on — band, rule, field — and the two combine cleanly: the figure lives in the field, the badge lives in the band. C is the safe crowd-pleaser, D is the one to reach for if the badge ever gets redrawn, and E is a mood board, not a proposal.

Geometry, mask, seals, sheen and hues are the live values from anago-leader-board-ui/src/styles/packopen.css and src/utils/packFoil.ts.
